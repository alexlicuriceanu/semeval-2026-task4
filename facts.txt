Contrastive learning is a machine learning technique where a model learns to understand data by comparing differences and similarities, rather than being told exactly what everything is.

Instead of learning to classify a specific label (e.g., "this image is a dog"), a contrastive model learns a general rule: "These two things are similar, and those two things are different."

To succeed in Track B, you will likely use a loss function like Triplet Margin Loss or InfoNCE (Contrastive Loss). You feed all three texts through your neural network to get three vectors. The math then calculates the error based on how close the Anchor is to the Positive compared to the Negative, and updates the model weights to correct it.
